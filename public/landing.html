The difference

Anyone can show you the sky today. We can prove what it did.

Public catalogues are a snapshot — they tell you where objects are right now and forget everything else. OrbitIQ writes every detection into a permanent intelligence archive at the moment it happens. That record cannot be reconstructed later from public sources, by us or by anyone else.

  • 01Every conjunction, congestion shift, re-entry and environmental event is archived at detection time.
  • 02Each entry carries the hash of the entry before it. Editing, deleting or reordering any record breaks the chain — and verification names the exact row where it broke.
  • 03The tip of the chain is published hourly to a public branch. GitHub timestamps those commits, so a seal published last March is independent evidence of what the archive held last March.
  • 04Trends are measured against what we actually observed, not back-filled from today’s catalogue.
  • 05The longer the platform runs, the less replaceable the history becomes.

Check it yourself, without an account: /api/archive/verify. An integrity claim only the vendor can test is not an integrity claim.

Stated plainly: this is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof. It shows the record has not been edited since a published seal. It is not a cryptographic signature and does not assert who wrote the rows.

“The value is not in knowing that two objects will pass close. It is in being able to prove, six months later, exactly what the sky looked like when you made the decision to manoeuvre.”
