How it works

Screen. Decide. Prove.

Most tools stop at the warning. OrbitIQ keeps going: it records what you decided about each one, binds that decision to the exact warning, and lets anyone you choose check the whole history later.

Every close approach, ranked by probability.

All-on-all screening against the public catalogue, refined to the time of closest approach, with a Foster 2D collision probability for each encounter. You see what threatens your spacecraft, not just what passes near it.

The record

Anyone can show you the sky today. We can show what it held.

Public catalogues are snapshots; they forget. OrbitIQ writes every detection into an archive at the moment it happens, and that history cannot be rebuilt later from public data — by us or by anyone.

  1. Archived at detection timeConjunctions, re-entries, congestion shifts and space-weather events are written when they are seen, not reconstructed afterwards.
  2. Chained, so edits showEach row carries the hash of the row before it. Changing, deleting or reordering any row breaks the chain, and verification names the row where it broke.
  3. Witnessed outside our serversThe tip of the chain is published to a public witness log, so there is a record of what the archive held that we cannot quietly revise.

Where OrbitIQ fits

Keep your data provider. Add the record.

Space safety has three layers. OrbitIQ is built for the one that is usually missing: a verifiable account of what you were told and what you did about it.

Layer 1

Tracking networks

Radar and optical sensor networks — government and commercial — that produce the observations, orbit solutions and conjunction data messages. Higher-accuracy data starts here.

Layer 2

Coordination

Platforms and services that deliver conjunction messages and help two operators agree who manoeuvres. Essential when both objects can move.

Layer 3 · OrbitIQ

The record

An independent screen on public data, a probability for every approach, a decision log bound to each warning, and an archive a third party can verify without an account.

Not a replacement for your CDMs

Screening runs on public general-perturbation elements with kilometre-level uncertainty. For a manoeuvre decision, use your provider's conjunction data messages too.

Not a compliance verdict

OrbitIQ holds evidence that supports some obligations and says plainly which it cannot touch. It never tells you that you are compliant.

Not tamper-proof

Tamper-evident. If a row is altered, verification shows it and names the row. The external witness is as strong as GitHub's timestamps.

Not a black box

The models, thresholds and calibration method are documented, and every public figure has an endpoint behind it you can call yourself.

Questions

Straight answers.

How accurate is the screening?

Positions come from public GP element sets (CelesTrak, refreshed every six hours) propagated with SGP4, so uncertainty is kilometre-level and grows with element age. Collision probability uses a covariance scaled to element age, and the scaling is checked against measured element-set residuals. It is built for monitoring, screening and triage — not as the sole basis for an operational manoeuvre.

What objects are in the catalogue?

All active satellites plus the four large catalogued debris clouds CelesTrak publishes as their own groups (Cosmos 1408, Fengyun-1C, Cosmos 2251 and Iridium 33). The live counts are at the top of this page and in the live sky.

How does someone verify our record without trusting you?

Every row in the archive carries the hash of the one before it, and the chain's tip is published hourly to a public witness log. /api/v1/archive/verify walks the whole chain and reports any row that does not reconcile. A Proof of Diligence report carries the hashes and plain instructions so a reader can check it with no account.

How is this different from LeoLabs or Slingshot?

LeoLabs operates its own radar network and Slingshot its own sensors and the Beacon coordination platform; both produce or move higher-accuracy tracking data than public elements can give. OrbitIQ does not compete on sensors. It focuses on the record: a decision log bound to each warning, a verifiable archive, and scoped access for insurers and regulators. It can sit alongside either.

Who can see our fleet and decisions?

Only your workspace. A verifier — an insurer, a regulator, a prime — sees your record only through a grant you issue: scoped to what you choose, time-boxed, revocable, and read-only by construction.

Can we run it ourselves?

Yes. OrbitIQ is MIT-licensed and runs as a single Node.js service with no paid dependencies. Institutional plans cover a dedicated instance, private catalogue ingestion and named support.
